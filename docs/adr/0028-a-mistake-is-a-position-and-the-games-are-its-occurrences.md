# A 错题 is a position, and the games you lost it in are its 遭遇

老毛病 identified each finding by the file it came from and the Ply it sat at. That is the right
identity for "what went wrong in this game" and the wrong one for a book you practise out of: play
the same opening four times and blunder the same way each time, and it yields four findings that
look identical and know nothing about each other.

**A 错题 is a position.** Four games, one 错题, with four 遭遇 hanging off it — when it happened,
which game, what was played, what it cost, where the game came from. Identity is the placement,
the side to move, the castling rights and the en-passant square, and nothing else: the half-move
clock and the move number are not part of what a position *is*, and including them would split
the same position reached by different move orders into strangers.

The payoff is a sentence no other identity can produce: **「这个局面你栽过 4 次，三次走 Nf3，一次
走 Bd3，最近一次是 3 天前」**. 复发 — falling for the same position again — turns out to be the most
useful thing the app knows about a player, more useful than how badly any single move scored, and
it only exists if the four times are one thing.

Keying on the position *and the wrong move* was the other candidate. It was rejected because it
splits on the wrong axis: two different bad moves in one position is one hole in the player's
understanding, not two, and the distinction is not lost — it is in the 遭遇 list, where it can be
read without being used to divide the item.

## Consequences

- 遭遇 from different sources merge. A position blundered in a rated game and then again in 耕棋
  is **one 错题 that got worse**, not two. This is the point, not a collision.
- 复发 is the only thing allowed to jump the enrolment queue, whatever it cost. Twelve percent
  three times says the player has no concept here; forty percent once may only say they were
  tired.
- A 错题 has no single date. "When did this happen" is a property of a 遭遇, and any summary of it
  — first, last, how often — is arithmetic over the list.
